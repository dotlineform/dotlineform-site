# Flat Tag Identity Concept

## Purpose

Keep tag data limited to the identity needed to assign a tag to work and series
records. Do not encode interpretative distinctions such as subject, domain,
form, or theme as identity namespaces or conventional attributes.

## Mental model

`moss` is the tag. A document may explain moss as depicted material, ecological
domain, metaphor, theme, or any other useful context. Those readings are prose,
not separate canonical tags and not analytical group membership.

```text
flat tag identity
  -> assigned to work and series records
  -> linked one way to one explanatory document
  -> interpreted through document content
```

## Invariants

- `tag_id` is the globally unique normalized tag name.
- The registry has neither `group` nor `groups`, and tag identity has no group
  prefix.
- Group is not retained for filtering, scoring, coverage, styling, alias
  constraints, or report metadata.
- Contextual differences between previously namespaced instances are resolved
  in one document rather than represented as parallel registry rows.
- Assignment data records the tag only; it does not record which interpretation
  motivated an assignment.
- The registry remains the assignment identity authority.
- After Tag-document linking, the associated document is the semantic
  authority; registry prose is removed rather than synchronized.
- The registry holds the one-way `tag_id` to `doc_id` relationship. Documents
  do not carry a `tag_id` back-reference, and unrelated documents may remain
  unreferenced.

## Collision decision

Labels currently repeated across namespaces are not mechanically collapsed.
Each is reviewed manually to choose one flat identity, its assignments and
aliases, and document content that can carry the useful contextual distinctions.

The migration may preserve several genuinely different tags only by giving
them genuinely different names. It must not recreate the removed group model
through suffixes, hidden attributes, compatibility aliases, or report-only
classification.
