# Flat Tag Identity Concept

## Purpose

Keep the identity used by work and series assignments independent from tag
classification. Subject, domain, form, and theme remain allowed analytical
groups, but they are not identity namespaces.

## Mental model

`moss` is the tag and `subject` is its one group. A document may explain moss as
depicted material, ecological domain, metaphor, theme, or any other useful
context. Those readings are prose, not separate canonical tags or additional
group memberships.

```text
flat tag identity
  -> classified by one registry group
  -> assigned to work and series records
  -> linked one way to one explanatory document
  -> interpreted through document content
```

## Invariants

- `tag_id` is the globally unique normalized tag name.
- Every registry tag has exactly one required scalar `group` from the allowed
  group policy. A `groups` array is not supported.
- Tag identity has no group prefix, and no consumer infers group from `tag_id`.
- Filtering, scoring, coverage, styling, alias constraints, and report metadata
  resolve group through the registry record.
- Contextual differences between previously namespaced instances are resolved
  in one document rather than represented as parallel registry rows.
- Assignment data records the tag only; it does not record which interpretation
  motivated an assignment or duplicate the registry group.
- The registry remains the assignment identity authority.
- Changing a tag's group does not change `tag_id` or rewrite assignments, but it
  is a validated registry mutation because it can affect aliases, reports, and
  scoring.
- After Tag-document linking, the associated document is the semantic
  authority; registry prose is removed rather than synchronized.
- The registry holds the one-way `tag_id` to `doc_id` relationship. Documents
  do not carry a `tag_id` back-reference, and unrelated documents may remain
  unreferenced.

## Collision decision

Labels repeated across namespaces are not mechanically collapsed. Each
collision is reviewed to choose one flat identity, exactly one group, its
assignments and aliases, and document content that can carry the useful
contextual distinctions.

The migration may preserve several genuinely different tags only by giving
them genuinely different names, with one group for each resulting tag. It must
not recreate namespaced identity through compatibility prefixes or aliases.

Removing a registry `label` that duplicates the flat `tag_id` is a separate
schema decision. It is not required to flatten identity.
