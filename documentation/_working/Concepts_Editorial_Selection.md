# Concepts Editorial Selection

An existential question runs through these works. What makes a life, a gesture or an image matter when the world offers no assurance that it will? In *will we leave residues*, the speaker imagines leaving behind traces as small as particles of soil in water. Would anyone see a pattern in what remains? A mark can outlast the person who left it. Coming to mean something to someone is a further event. [will we leave residues](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20260215-000000-b24b4f)

We need to make meaning because the question of what matters enters ordinary decisions. We have limited time, and we keep having to decide what to care for, what to carry from the past, and what is worth doing before we know how it will turn out. Drawing a line, keeping a photograph or putting a memory into words gives something we feel a form we can return to. We can look at it again, change our minds about it or offer it to someone else.

In *we are the vessel*, the search for Plato’s Forms becomes a way to speak about loneliness, longing and love. The poem suggests that the shared experience of exploring might itself be what we were looking for. A work can give two people something to attend to together. They may come away with different accounts of what they saw, and the difference can give them a reason to keep looking. The Concepts make room for that kind of exchange. [we are the vessel](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20260215-000000-c27a38)

These forty working notes connect Catalogue works, texts and Library readings. A photograph from *a doll story* can be evidence of what remains, an object a memory gathers around or a presence that seems to return. The same image can therefore belong to **Trace**, **Memory** and **Ghost**. Words like *trace*, *fold* and *vessel* can likewise lead in several directions at once.

The reading notes set these questions beside existentialism and writing on the absurd, historical alchemy and Jung’s readings of it, and Buddhist or Daoist texts. A difference between a source and a work can give us as much to follow as a resemblance.

## Absence

An empty place can still organise the things around it. The titles *nothing is missing* and *untitled (destroyed)* raise the question of how we come to feel that something should be there, even if we have never seen it. In a picture, an unused area may begin to look like a deliberate gap. In a life, a habit can outlast the thing it formed around. What makes an omission palpable in either case? [Nothing is missing](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=01448) · [Untitled [destroyed]](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=01883)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* In chapter 11 of the *Daodejing*, the hole in a wheel hub and the openings in a room help make those things usable. An opening in an image can also make something possible. What can be seen or imagined in the space it leaves? [Daodejing, 11](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/T%C3%A2o_Teh_King#11)

## Ambiguity

A *trace* might be what remains, a faint amount, or a route we try to follow. A word or image can sustain several readings even when it is perfectly legible. In *3 symbols*, the photographic and symbolic layers offer different ways to read the same image. Some ambiguities resolve with more context. Others are the reason we keep looking. Where does openness to several meanings meet uncertainty about evidence or a measured quantity? [3 symbols](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20260801-212422-df11f3)

**Reading note.** *Existentialism.* Simone de Beauvoir gives ambiguity a wider sense than a word with several meanings. We can act within circumstances we did not choose, and other people can experience our actions in ways we cannot control. A work takes on a similar openness when someone else takes it up and finds a meaning its maker could not foresee. [The Ethics of Ambiguity, part I](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch01.htm)

## Attention

What does the eye return to after its first pass over an image? A marginal detail might take over, or looking for one thing might make something else disappear. In *10,000*, a single dot of colour, the space around it and the arrangement as a whole draw the eye in different ways. The same image may already look different a day later. Does knowing what to look for change what else becomes visible? [10,000](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20260801-212422-43b47c)

**Reading note.** *Buddhism.* The *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta* directs attention to the arising and passing of what we observe. Looking at an image has its own shifts of attention. A detail comes forward, holds the eye for a time and then gives way to something else. What changes when those shifts become noticeable? [Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta, MN 10](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN10.html)

## Balance

The print of *balance machine 1* gives us one arrangement to hold in view. The film raises questions about how long an equilibrium lasts and what it takes to find it again. The word carries us from weight and counterweight to the emotional effort of staying upright. How much movement can a state of balance contain? [Print](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=04813) · [Film](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=05248)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* Chapter 77 of the *Daodejing* uses the bending of a bow to picture taking from excess and supplying what is deficient. The image makes balance an adjustment. Where does a correction take place in *balance machine 1*, and what happens to the rest of the arrangement when it does? [Daodejing, 77](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/T%C3%A2o_Teh_King#77)

## Chance

The dice in Catalogue change the order in which works are encountered. In *3 symbols*, intuitive selections, incidental limits and a predetermined procedure helped make an image. Chance can enter at different points in making or looking. Which decisions are handed over to it, and which begin when the result appears? The *I Ching* and the Library’s writing on random numbers offer further ways to ask how an encounter comes to mean something. [Catalogue randomness](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20261113-101744-c4dd8f) · [3 symbols](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20260801-212422-df11f3)

**Reading note.** *Jung.* In his foreword to the Wilhelm–Baynes *I Ching*, Jung describes how a cast of coins enters into the interpretation of the moment. The cast and the Catalogue shuffle both produce an encounter. How much of its significance comes from what happens to turn up, and how much from the situation of the person reading it? [Jung’s foreword to the I Ching, collected works vol. 11](https://jungiancenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/vol-11-psychology-and-religion-west-and-east.pdf) · [Jung × I Ching in the Library](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261121-190054-070cca)

## Clarity

*Through a glass clearly* and *fog* raise a question about what becomes legible when detail is softened. Optical sharpness, the coherence of a composition and the ease of following an idea can come apart. Defocus might make a relation clearer by making the things it connects less distinct. The Library’s discussion of coherence and decoherence gives those words a precise use in physics. What do they suggest by analogy about the way an image holds together or comes apart? [Through a glass clearly](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=01763) · [Decoherence, interference and quantum computation](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261027-164336-ceaab1)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* Chapter 15 of the *Daodejing* asks how muddy water becomes clear as it settles. In an image, clarity might likewise come from waiting or giving the eye less to do. What comes into view when we let the image settle, and what comes into view when we actively bring a detail into focus? [Daodejing, 15](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/T%C3%A2o_Teh_King#15)

## Constraint

In *3 symbols*, working in threes and compositing images by a fixed procedure shaped the result alongside practical limits, including the number of folders that fitted on a screen. A choice can begin as an accommodation to a tool and become part of a work’s structure. What would changing one of those constraints have made possible? What distinctive quality might have disappeared with it? [3 symbols](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20260801-212422-df11f3)

**Reading note.** *Existentialism.* In “Freedom and Liberation,” Beauvoir examines what it takes for someone to have actual possibilities for action. Art-making raises a smaller version of that question. Some rules can be changed by the maker. Others come from conditions that determine what choices are available in the first place. [The Ethics of Ambiguity, part III, §2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch03.htm)

## Doll

In *Nerve*, Doll is given a body that can hold fears and desires it cannot experience for itself. In *a doll story*, a physical doll is photographed and shattered, and the images remain. The imagined vessel, the breakable object and the surviving photograph each offer a different way of encountering Doll. What kind of intimacy is possible with a figure that can be entrusted with things we find difficult to say and can never answer? [Nerve](/docs/?collection=texts&doc=d-20261127-100053-b77c4f) · [a doll story](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20260801-212422-3a2da4)

**Reading note.** *Alchemy and Jung.* In *Psychology and Alchemy*, Jung proposes that an alchemist might unwittingly experience something from their own inner life as a quality of the material they are working with. Doll seems to hold feelings the speaker of *Nerve* finds hard to voice. Where do those feelings come from, and what happens to them when they are given a body? [Psychology and Alchemy, §§345–347](https://www.jungiananalysts.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/C.-G.-Jung-Collected-Works-Volume-12_-Psychology-and-Alchemy.pdf)

## Dream

Near the end of *Nerve*, a place feels familiar even though the author describes never having visited it. In *dream sleep*, the word *dream* also means an aspiration, and a lost ball bearing is vividly remembered. An invented place, a recollection and a wish can inhabit the same scene. What gives such a scene the feeling of somewhere we have been, and why can that feeling outlast the details? [Nerve Context note](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20260801-073846-e0a9ec) · [dream sleep](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20260215-000000-1de7e9)

**Reading note.** *Jung and Daoism.* Jung follows images across a series of dreams in *Psychology and Alchemy*. The butterfly story in the *Zhuangzi* turns on a different question about who is dreaming whom. Both offer ways into the recurring place in *Nerve*. What becomes familiar across its appearances, and whose experience do we take that familiarity to be? [Psychology and Alchemy, part II, §§44–50](https://www.jungiananalysts.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/C.-G.-Jung-Collected-Works-Volume-12_-Psychology-and-Alchemy.pdf) · [Zhuangzi, 2](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

## Embodiment

What can a body know by placing a hand on something or changing its position? The reach of a hand matters in making an ink drawing in *hand life*. The distance from a work in *body space* matters in seeing it. Both kinds of encounter raise a question that becomes stranger in *Nerve*. How do we come to attribute bodily feeling to a figure that has a shape and no sensation? [Body space 1](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00783) · [Embodied mind](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261025-122644-1612d9)

**Reading note.** *Existentialism.* Beauvoir begins *The Ethics of Ambiguity* with the difficulty of being a subject to oneself and an object in the lives of others. A work from *body space* raises an analogous question about position. What changes when we become aware of where we are looking from as well as where the body we see appears to be? [The Ethics of Ambiguity, part I](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch01.htm)

## Emergence

In *fireworks form* and *molecule forms*, there may be a point at which a larger shape comes into view while its components are still visible. The making of the arrangement and its recognition are two events. How did the larger form come about, and when did it become visible? The Library’s writing on self-organisation makes it possible to ask a further question about how much came from the artist’s decisions and how much arose from the arrangement itself. [Emergence — Patterns Without Essence](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261025-214500-1e384c)

**Reading note.** *Buddhism.* The *Kaccānagotta Sutta* sets out dependent arising in its account of suffering. The question about conditions has an echo in *molecule forms*. What relations need to be in place before a shape appears, and what happens to it if one of them changes? [Kaccānagotta Sutta, SN 12.15](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN12_15.html)

## Field

In *field I* and *grid fields*, a mark belongs to a larger area. The marks around it, the edges and the quieter parts of the image all affect how it reads. A field of view or colour can also suggest a field of influence. Can something at one edge make a difference to how we see a mark at the other? The Library’s note gives the physical meaning of the word a place in that comparison. [Field I](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=01047) · [Field (Physics)](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261028-113959-7459a8)

**Reading note.** *Buddhism.* In the *Dvaya Sutta*, visual consciousness arises in dependence on the eye and visible forms. The appearance of a painted field changes with the distance and position of the viewer. What belongs to the arrangement of marks, and what takes place in the encounter between that arrangement and the viewer? [Dvaya Sutta, SN 35.93](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN35_93.html)

## Fold

A fold can hide an area, leave a crease or bring distant parts of one surface together. *Folding forms* and *creases* give two ways to think about the operation and what remains visible afterwards. In a text or a memory, two passages or times can come into contact while the distance between them still exists. What new connection becomes visible at the fold? [Folding forms 1](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=01107)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* Chapter 2 of the *Zhuangzi* imagines a pivot from which what is called “this” or “that” can turn. A fold can likewise bring two parts of a surface into contact. Can a turn bring both sides into view, and what remains concealed when it does? [Zhuangzi, 2](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

## Form

The title *found forms* suggests that a form can be encountered before it is drawn or named. Alongside *fluid forms*, it raises the question of what makes a form recognisable when its outline, material or scale changes. *Form* can be something we recognise. *To form* can be something an artist, a material or a set of conditions does. The search for Plato’s Forms in *we are the vessel* gives this a personal stake. What are we looking for when we look for an ideal form? [Found form (2017-07-09)](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00404) · [we are the vessel](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20260215-000000-c27a38)

**Reading note.** *Existentialism and Mahāyāna Buddhism.* Sartre uses a paper knife to illustrate how a manufactured object is made with an intended purpose. The *Heart Sūtra* speaks of form as empty. In the company of *found forms*, the readings raise two questions. What can we recognise before we know a thing’s purpose? What would it mean to see a form without giving it a fixed essence? [Existentialism Is a Humanism](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm) · [Heart Sūtra, §1.6](https://84000.co/translation/toh21)

## Fragment

A numbered *fragment* has a place of its own in a series. A passage gathered into *Nerve* takes on new neighbours within the text. A fragment can send us looking for a missing whole, stand on its own or become part of something its first maker could never have anticipated. The Library’s writing on reconstructive memory extends that question from editing a text to putting a past together. [Fragment 01](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=01202) · [Reconstruction: A Concept of Memory](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261123-190028-dde272)

**Reading note.** *Jung.* The dream study in *Psychology and Alchemy* brings partial accounts together in chronological order. The fragments in *Nerve* also take on a sequence. Which features of a single passage become significant because of what now comes before or after it? [Psychology and Alchemy, part II, §§44–50](https://www.jungiananalysts.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/C.-G.-Jung-Collected-Works-Volume-12_-Psychology-and-Alchemy.pdf)

## Frame

The titles *frame (positive)* and *frame (negative)* raise a question about what belongs to the work and what belongs to its surround. A crop, a border, a caption or a neighbouring image can change what counts as part of the subject. In *doll cell [framed version]*, the frame adds a physical question about the relation between what is painted on the glass and what sits behind it. [Frame (positive)](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00012) · [Doll cell [framed version]](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00873)

**Reading note.** *Existentialism.* In “The Aesthetic Attitude,” Beauvoir examines what happens when someone approaches a situation as a detached spectator. A frame gives us a place to look from. What can we see from there, and which people or circumstances become easier to overlook? [The Ethics of Ambiguity, part III, §1](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch03.htm)

## Gesture

In *gesture in grey* and *hand life*, a mark can suggest the speed, pressure or hesitation of the movement that made it. The gestures by which people communicate care or refusal are open to interpretation too. How much of a movement’s character is available to somebody who sees it once, sees it repeated or meets it only in a drawing? [Gesture in grey 1](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00006) · [Hand life 1](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00167)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* Cook Ding in the *Zhuangzi* works by following the spaces in what he cuts. The story also shows him slowing down at a difficult place. A drawn line may carry comparable changes of pace. Where did the maker have to accommodate the material, and where did long practice let the movement proceed easily? [Zhuangzi, 3](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

## Ghost

In *we make ghosts*, ghosts pass through us and a wall of memories is continually rebuilt. In *a doll story*, the photographs can make the doll feel present after the object itself has gone. Where does that sense of presence come from? An afterimage, a recurrent dream and the impression that someone still inhabits a place each suggest a way we might supply something an image seems to contain. [we make ghosts](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20260215-000000-cb83ef)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* In chapter 2 of the *Zhuangzi*, the shadow explains its movements by pointing to something else on which it depends. The ghosts in *we make ghosts* raise a question about what sustains a presence. A place, an earlier encounter or someone who remembers might all help it endure. What would make it disappear? [Zhuangzi, 2](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

## Grid

In *10,000*, dots of colour inhabit a grid and the text insists on the space between them. In *grid dissection*, the grid itself becomes the subject of an operation. A grid gives each element an address, including an empty cell. That makes counting and comparison possible, and it gives the social analogy in *10,000* its force. The spacing that makes a group legible also keeps its members apart. [10,000](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20260801-212422-43b47c) · [Grid dissection](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00158)

**Reading note.** *Historical alchemy.* Michael Maier’s twenty-first emblem in *Atalanta fugiens* places a pair of figures inside a construction of circles, a square and a triangle. In both the emblem and *10,000*, a geometric arrangement gives things a place in relation to one another. What does each arrangement invite us to count, compare or imagine happening inside it? [Atalanta fugiens, emblem 21](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Michael_Maier_Atalanta_Fugiens_Emblem_21.jpeg)

## Home

The *home land loss* series gives home two neighbours that put pressure on the word. What belongs to a place, what belongs to the person who remembers it, and what travels when they leave? The domestic spaces of *a humid home full of light* and *boundary conditions* raise a related question about familiarity. A place can become home through acts repeated until they feel ordinary. How much of that life can be revisited in an image after either the place or the person has changed? [Home](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=01372) · [boundary conditions](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20260215-000000-c5e2d1)

**Reading note.** *Absurdism.* In *The Myth of Sisyphus*, Albert Camus associates the feeling of the absurd with becoming a stranger in a world that had seemed familiar. The *home land loss* works give that estrangement somewhere concrete to begin. What makes a setting feel like home, and how much of that feeling can an image bring back? [The Myth of Sisyphus, “Absurdity and Suicide”](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/blogs.umb.edu/dist/5/6359/files/2023/07/The-Myth-of-Sisyphus-Camus.pdf)

## Hope

In *Nerve*, the search through painful material eventually yields a jewel that can absorb and reform suffering. *Memory loss hope* holds those elements together in a different form. In *moments in an unfinished painting*, the work is scratched and worked over while its outcome is still uncertain. What does hope allow the maker to risk, and how does a work show signs of it while keeping its scars? [Nerve](/docs/?collection=texts&doc=d-20261127-100053-b77c4f) · [moments in an unfinished painting](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20261024-095656-151301)

**Reading note.** *Existentialism.* Beauvoir’s “The Present and the Future” asks how a project directed towards the future can honour the lives and actions taking place now. A painting that may remain unfinished gives that question a particular form. Where is the value of staying with it experienced today, and how does the idea of its future change the work being done now? [The Ethics of Ambiguity, part III, §4](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch03.htm)

## Interval

In *train moments*, the timestamps mark individual captures while leaving the time between them to the reader. In a film such as *by the river*, time passes while the viewer looks. A pause between lines in Moments can hold time open in yet another way. An interval can be a gap, a distance or a span in which something happens. What changes in the relation between two images or two lines when more time passes between them? [Train moments Work](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=01770) · [By the river](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=05058)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* Chapter 16 of the *Daodejing* puts stillness alongside observing things as they return to their roots. A pause in a poem or film might likewise give us time to recognise a movement already underway. What becomes possible to notice when the interval is long enough? [Daodejing, 16](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/T%C3%A2o_Teh_King#16)

## Line

A line can divide a surface, carry the eye across it, record where a hand went or give a voice a place to pause. The curves in *curve machine* or *curve poems* and the arranged lines of *all the little cracks* give the eye different paths to follow. A line can also become a route, a sentence or part of a diagram. Its beginning and end may matter as much as the path it takes, especially when it leaves a connection for the reader to continue. [Curve machine](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00867) · [all the little cracks](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20260215-000000-f5f727)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* Chapter 27 of the *Daodejing* describes a skilled traveller who leaves no tracks. A line in *curve poems* can suggest a journey even when the route by which it was made is impossible to reconstruct. Where does the eye travel when the original movement is unavailable to it? [Daodejing, 27](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/T%C3%A2o_Teh_King#27) · [Tao and Art in the Library](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261124-182224-50ae84)

## Memory

In *forest memories 1-48*, many images are gathered under one title. In the Moment *memory*, photographs are imagined as continually in motion. What becomes different each time an image is revisited, selected or set alongside another? A photograph can settle a detail in a remembered scene and reopen the question of what the scene meant. The Library’s work on reconstructive memory asks what happens between one recollection of an event and the next. [Forest memories 1-48](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00464) · [memory](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20260215-000000-cd5956) · [Reconstruction: A Concept of Memory](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261123-190028-dde272)

**Reading note.** *Jung and alchemy.* The Library’s *Jung’s Alchemy × Memory* explores recollection through images of dissolving and recombining. When an image from *forest memories 1-48* brings a scene to mind, what is altered in the recollection? What does the language of alchemy let us describe about that alteration? [Jung’s Alchemy × Memory in the Library](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261121-185036-ebf486)

## Model

The *kylie wax model* series and the imperfect lens imagined in *10,000* offer different ways to approach a model. It can be an object made to study another thing, a possible world built to see what happens inside it or a simplification that lets a relation come into view. Handling wax, looking at a picture and changing an assumption in a mathematical model each make different discoveries possible. When do the conveniences of a model start to look like properties of the thing it was made to explain? [Kylie wax model 1.0](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=01394) · [10,000](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20260801-212422-43b47c)

**Reading note.** *Buddhism.* In the *Alagaddūpama Sutta*, a raft is useful because it gets someone across and can be left after the crossing. The lens in *10,000* raises a similar question about the life of a tool. What has a model made possible, and at what point might carrying it into the next enquiry get in the way? [Alagaddūpama Sutta, MN 22](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN22.html)

## Nerve

The word can mean a pathway for sensation, a tender point or the courage to do something. All three senses meet in a reading of *Nerve* and *nerve forms*. Where does a sensation travel, where does it become an image, and where does the speaker find the nerve to put it into words? The Library’s account of bodily signalling sits alongside the felt experience of being touched, shocked or exposed. [Nerve](/docs/?collection=texts&doc=d-20261127-100053-b77c4f) · [Brain signals](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261018-105902-482d5b)

**Reading note.** *Buddhism.* In the discourse on the two arrows, one arrow represents bodily pain and the second the mental distress that can follow. In *Nerve*, a sensation can acquire a memory, a fear or a story. The arrows offer a way of asking how these experiences come together, even where the passage leaves them difficult to distinguish. [Sallattha Sutta, SN 36.6](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN36_6.html)

## Pattern

In *all the little cracks*, what first seems to be damage starts to reveal small patterns. How do we decide that several occurrences belong together, and what do we do with one that almost fits? Pattern can be made by following a rule, noticed in something that has already happened or projected when a few details are all we have. The Library’s work on pattern separation and completion offers one way to pursue the question of what recognition supplies. [all the little cracks](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20260215-000000-f5f727) · [Pattern Separation vs Completion](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261123-133223-a3f2be)

**Reading note.** *Jung.* Jung’s foreword to the *I Ching* considers the significance an observer finds in a coincidence. In *all the little cracks*, the appearance of a pattern changes how the speaker reads what first seemed like damage. What would count as finding the same pattern again, and what would count as a different reading of it? [Jung’s foreword to the I Ching, collected works vol. 11](https://jungiancenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/vol-11-psychology-and-religion-west-and-east.pdf)

## Repair

In *moments in an unfinished painting*, scraping, dissolving and scrubbing leave scars. The title *birth of healing forms* raises the possibility that a repair can bring something new into being. Hiding a break, joining it visibly and making a new work from what survived each imply a different idea of what needs to be restored. How far can those ideas travel from mending an object to tending to a person? [Birth of healing forms](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00782) · [moments in an unfinished painting](/docs/?collection=moments&doc=d-20261024-095656-151301)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* Chapter 79 of the *Daodejing* allows that ill feeling can persist after a dispute is settled. In *moments in an unfinished painting*, the marks of earlier interventions remain visible. What has been mended, what has been put back into use, and what persists after the repair? ## Repetition

In a sequence from *certainty* or *reduce and return*, some variations become visible only after several works. Repeating a movement or a line of writing can also make a small departure easier to notice. How does the act of repeating change what the artist or viewer expects to happen? The return can change the person who repeats it as well as the thing being repeated. [Certainty](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=00389) · [Imperfect Repetition Is the Condition for Growth](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261121-165922-8c6edf)

**Reading note.** *Absurdism.* Camus dwells on Sisyphus walking back down the mountain to his stone. The return is a moment of awareness within the recurring task. In a sequence from *certainty* or *reduce and return*, there is also a space between one work and the next. How does going back change the person doing the repeating? ## Scale

In *10,000*, the imagined act of magnifying a particle produces still more particles and eventually a mist. A work and a detail from it pose a related question about which relations survive a change of view. A detail may need a vocabulary quite different from the one that described the whole. Does something similar happen when an instant in a film is stretched out in time? [10,000](/docs/?collection=works&doc=d-20260801-212422-43b47c) · [Perception’s Sweet Spot of Scale](/docs/?collection=library&doc=d-20261123-133628-b6e4a8)

**Reading note.** *Daoism.* In “Autumn Floods,” the *Zhuangzi* moves from a river to the sea and to a frog whose world is a well. Each change of scale alters what can be taken in. What seems complete from one position, and what requires us to move or learn another way of describing it? [Zhuangzi, 17](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

## Signal

*Nerve* opens with a sound through fog that might be a foghorn or a siren. Before we know what it means, it makes us attend. Together, the opening and *particle signals* raise a question about what makes a disturbance reach someone as news, warning or invitation. Noise can obscure a signal, supply context or become the thing a different listener came to hear. Who or what decides when a disturbance counts as a message? [Nerve](/docs/?collection=texts&doc=d-20261127-100053-b77c4f) · [Particle signals 1 a](/docs/?collection=catalogue&doc=04629)

**Reading note.** *Buddhism.* The instruction to Bāhiya asks him to attend to what is heard in the hearing itself. In *Nerve*, what has reached the listener before they call it a foghorn or a siren? ## Surface

*Surface history* and the layers and scarring in *moments in an unfinished painting* suggest that what meets the eye all at once can hold evidence of actions spread over years. A layer of paint, a sheet of glass and skin each let some things through and hold others back. How much of what we call depth is available through the surface, and what do we imagine from the marks it carries? ## Symbol

In *3 symbols*, forms associated with birth, death and healing can be combined until their components are hard to pick out. ## Threshold

In the Moment *boundary conditions*, the room can be described with certainty while time slips its usual frame. *Boundary conditions (by the river)* gives the idea another setting. ## Trace

Trace of what? In *will we leave residues*, the question is whether what remains will have a pattern someone can read. A trace can be a minute quantity, a mark left by contact, a line made by following another line or a route reconstructed from surviving signs. A work from *residues* and a photograph from *a doll story* each give us something different to follow. Where does the trail go cold, and what does the person doing the tracing leave behind in turn? **Reading note.** *Buddhism.* In the *Khemaka Sutta*, washed cloth still carries a scent from the washing. Khemaka uses that residue to speak about a sense of “I am” that lingers after he has ceased to identify himself with any of the five aggregates. In *3 symbols*, adding a new concept can remake a whole symbol. In *Nerve*, Doll is imagined as a means of giving suffering another form. ## Uncertainty

## Vessel

In *little clear vessel*, the vessel is empty and open and somehow contains a couple’s love. *Memory loss vessels* and the body made for Doll give other forms to the idea of holding something invisible. ## Voice

In *Nerve*, Doll cannot speak and the writing gives her things to say that the speaker cannot. Who seems to be talking in a passage of automatic writing? **Reading note.** *Buddhism.* In the discourse to Prince Abhaya, truth, benefit and timing matter when someone decides whether to speak.
